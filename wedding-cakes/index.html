What a Slovak sweet table is

At a Slovak wedding nobody waits for one cake to be cut. A long table is laid with trays of small sweets — punch slices, meringue shells, walnut crescents, cream puffs, Linzer cookies — and guests help themselves all night.

It is generous, it photographs beautifully, and it solves the problem of feeding a room full of people with different tastes. Katka builds it to the number of guests and the length of the reception, and provides tiers, tongs and boxes for leftovers.

How it works

Ordering, step by step

1. Tell Katka

Send the date, the occasion, roughly how many people and any flavour you have your heart set on.

2. Agree the design

Katka replies with options, a sketch if it helps, and a firm price. Nothing is locked in until you are happy.

3. Confirm with a deposit

A 30% deposit holds your date. Popular weekends fill up several weeks ahead.

4. Collect it fresh

Your order is baked in the two days before pickup and handed over boxed, with serving notes.
