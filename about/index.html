It started with a walnut grinder

I grew up in a house where the oven was never really off. My grandmother baked on Fridays, and my job — from about the age of five — was to turn the handle of her cast-iron walnut grinder until my arm ached. She kept her recipes in a squared exercise book, written in pencil, with butter fingerprints on the page for medovník.

That notebook came with me to Australia.

Why the folk heart

The blue flowers and red hearts on my logo are the embroidery patterns from the region my family is from. They are stitched onto tablecloths, painted onto houses, and they are the first thing I think of when I think of home. It seemed right to put them on the box your cake arrives in.

How I bake now

Out of my own kitchen on the Gold Coast, in small batches, to order. I do not freeze anything and I do not use premixes or preservatives — which means I need notice, and it means I can only take so many orders in a week. I would rather bake fifteen good things than fifty average ones.

Most weeks I am making a birthday cake, a tray of buchty for a Slovak family who misses home, and a box of zákusky for someone Australian who tried medovník at a friend’s place and has been thinking about it ever since.

What I promise

Four things I will not cut corners on

Real butter

No margarine, no vegetable cream, no shortening. It costs more and it tastes like it.

Nothing frozen

Your order is baked in the two days before you collect it. Yeast dough, the same morning.

An honest yes or no

If a design will not hold up in Queensland heat, or a date is too tight, I will say so.

The real recipe

Medovník rests two days. Kremeš is made the morning of. Some things cannot be rushed.
